A network that reads handwriting by settling.

A handwritten digit becomes a row of bits. The bits flip one at a time, each flip downhill on an energy slope shaped by a few stored patterns, until nothing moves. Where the bits come to rest is the answer. It uses whole numbers only, so the same digit settles to the same bits on any machine. Every bar was sealed before a line of code, and it was tested once on handwriting from 13 people it never saw.

What this is

This is Rung 1 of a ladder toward computing by geometry instead of by large models. The question it answers is simple: can a network do a real task by settling, deterministically, robustly to noise, on cases it has never seen? The task is reading a handwritten digit. The cell is a small dense associative memory: a few stored patterns per digit, an energy that rewards being close to them, and a settle that only ever goes downhill.

It is not the best digit reader. A plain nearest-neighbour search over every training digit is shown beside it for that reason. What it adds is the shape of the computation: whole numbers, a guaranteed stop, a measurable fit for every answer, and a size small enough for later rungs to put on a chip.

How it works

Step by step

  1. Bits. Each of the 64 ink counts becomes a few bits by a thermometer code: a bit is on when the ink is at least its threshold. Hamming distance between two digits is then a coarse measure of how different their ink is.
  2. The shape. For each digit 0 to 9, a few patterns are grown from the training digits by deterministic k-modes: cluster the class, take each cluster's bitwise majority. Every tie goes to the lowest index, so the shape is the same on every run.
  3. The energy. Every stored pattern within reach R of the state pulls on it, more strongly the closer it is, raised to the power n. The energy is minus the total pull. All of it is whole numbers, held below 253 so nothing rounds.
  4. The settle. Sweep the bits in order. Flip a bit only if the energy strictly drops. Stop after a sweep in which nothing flips. Because every flip lowers the energy, the settle has to stop.
  5. The answer. At the resting state, add up the pull from each class's patterns. The class with the most is the answer. If nothing pulls at all, there is no answer, and no answer counts as wrong.
  6. The fit. The total pull at rest is the fit. A real digit settles into a deep well. A scrambled one, with the same ink in the wrong places, finds almost nothing to settle into. That measured non-fit is the groundwork for Rung 5, where it becomes a defence.

    White paper: computing by settling, on a real task, with the failures kept

    The question. Large models compute by pushing numbers through layers. An older idea computes by settling: a state rolls downhill in an energy landscape until it rests, and the resting place is the answer (Hopfield, 1982). Dense associative memories sharpen the energy so that many patterns can be stored without blurring together (Krotov and Hopfield, 2016). Rung 1 asks whether a cell like that can do a real task deterministically, survive noise, and hold up on cases it never saw.

    The method. UCI's handwritten digits: 3,823 training digits from 30 writers and 1,797 test digits from 13 different writers. The claims and bars were sealed first. Settings were chosen on a dev split of the training file only. The chosen cell was frozen by hash (seal 2) before the test file entered the repository, and the test was read once.

    What happened on the way. The first sealed grid used powers up to 4. On dev, that energy was too soft: many moderately close patterns together outweighed the nearest one, and the settle slid into a mixture of digits. The search was widened to sharper powers before the test was read. No bar moved, and the first grid's cell was also run on the test so its cost is visible.

      The limits, plainly:

      • One dataset, one task: isolated, size-normalised digits. Finding and cutting out digits on a real form is a separate step that this rung does not do.
      • Nearest-neighbour over all 3,823 training digits is the strong reference, and the dataset's authors report 98.00% for it on the raw features. The cell keeps far fewer patterns. The point is the shape of the computation, not the top score.
      • The dev search was amended once, before the test was read, and that is disclosed above. The test file has been read for these two cells only.
      • Rung 1 is not self-improvement (Rung 2), not a defence (Rung 5), and not on a chip (Rung 7). Whole numbers are chosen so those rungs can follow.
